# Clear Technical English

Make technical explanations easy to read and review. Apply the spirit of Simplified Technical English: direct sentences, consistent terminology and clear actions. Keep the requested detail without polite filler or repetition.

## Words and sentences

1. Prefer familiar words with a clear meaning in context. Use one term consistently for each thing. Do not introduce synonyms just to make the writing sound varied.
2. Keep established technical terms, product names and company terminology. Explain unfamiliar terms briefly when needed.
3. Replace long groups of nouns with a clearer phrase. Define a short form when a long official term must remain.
4. Use direct verbs instead of phrases such as perform an installation. Keep verb constructions simple.
5. Use short, complete sentences with one main idea. Keep words that clarify the relationship between ideas.
6. Prefer active voice when the actor is known. Passive voice can be useful in a description when the actor is unknown.

## Instructions and explanations

Give ordered actions as numbered steps. Put a condition before the action when the reader needs it first. Give one action per instruction when practical; keep genuinely simultaneous actions together. Keep instructions out of informational notes.

For an existing direct instruction, use a direct verb. Preserve the strength of a recommendation or permission: do not change should or may into must or a command. This is an intentional adaptation for everyday engineering writing.

For explanations, introduce information gradually. Keep each paragraph on one topic and connect related ideas. Do not turn a description into an instruction just to shorten it.

Keep sentences and paragraphs comfortably short. The official limits are described in [writing-notes.md](writing-notes.md); use them as readability targets here, not reasons to remove necessary detail.

When rewriting an existing warning, preserve its label, condition, risk and required action. Keep company controlled wording unchanged unless the user asks to edit it. Do not add warnings as a formatting habit.

## Preserve meaning

Simplify wording without changing technical meaning, conditions, uncertainty or obligation. Keep code, commands, identifiers, quantities, units and quotations exact. Rebuild an unclear sentence rather than making a misleading word substitution.

Keep the answer easy to scan without turning every sentence into a heading or list. Follow the user's format, spelling and style preferences. Return only the rewrite when that is requested. Do not add a facts audit, operational plan or open questions because this skill is active.
