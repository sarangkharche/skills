# Writing notes

Use these notes when the distinction between this practical guide and strict STE matters.

## Two kinds of technical writing

A procedure tells a reader what to do. A description explains an item, system or situation. Choose the appropriate sentence structure instead of giving every answer the shape of a runbook.

In this adaptation, preserve the source's meaning and the user's requested format. For example, a recommendation remains a recommendation even when a command would be shorter.

## Readability targets

Issue 9 distinguishes procedures from descriptions. Its sentence limits are 20 words for procedures and 25 for descriptions. It also limits a descriptive paragraph to six sentences. These are optional targets in this skill. Do not claim a strict word count check without applying the standard's counting conventions. Do not count executable code as prose or alter a command to shorten a sentence.

## Vocabulary

The full standard controls a word's meaning, grammatical role and permitted forms. It also accommodates technical nouns and technical verbs. A model's familiar sounding synonym is not proof that the standard approves it. This package does not contain the dictionary or check every word against it.

Use precise company terms consistently in ordinary engineering communication. Do not replace established terms with broader words simply to sound simpler.

## Intentional adaptations

This skill respects user and company choices on spelling, contractions and punctuation. Strict STE has more detailed restrictions in these areas. This guide does not enforce all of them. It also preserves the source's degree of obligation rather than treating every recommendation as an imperative.

The website and AI white paper explain that language tools assist writers and do not establish formal compliance. That boundary is documented here without adding a new reasoning audit to ordinary answers.

## The full article's contribution

The practical aim is to reduce reading effort when reviewing AI output. Consistent naming is more useful here than stylistic variety. Use short direct wording without stripping away the technical explanation. The article's visual examples are a separate idea, not a requirement to generate software or video for every answer.
